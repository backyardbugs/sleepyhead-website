I kept the midi files, but I don't have the VSTs in the channel strips, so I can't show you what I was doing there. I'm sure for subbass I either used SubLabXL or Serum. For the track labeled bfingers, I used Waves Bass Fingers. There was a really weird bass preset that sounded good only in context and only in the chorus. It might have been the Kublai preset with wah or the Chicken preset, I'm not sure. All I know is it sounded really weird and funky and when it ducked with the kick I liked it. I wanted to have a little bass moment, so I tracked some bass for verse 2.

<figure class="blog-media">
  <img src="img/mix-breakdown-ricky-ricardo/bass-parallax.png" alt="Tracked bass with Parallax">
</figure>

I'm using Parallax. It's the pop bass preset with a lot of the low end cut out and mids pushed. I just wanted it to be a crunchy picky bass to feel really separate from the sub bass that comes in. The tracking is messy. I'm not the tightest bass player. But, I actually find that because the programmed stuff feels so gridded, it allows my messier tracked stuff to give the song back some life. If everything was tracked this messy, I don't think it would feel good, but in the context of having that quantized feel in the drums under it, I think it works.

### Candy and texture

Now the candy and texture stuff.

<figure class="blog-media blog-audio">
  <audio controls preload="none" src="audio/RickyRicardo_candy.mp3"></audio>
</figure>

This is arguably my favorite part of arranging a song. I love adding weird samples and pads and textures that transport the listener. Like, there's this Julia Wolf song with a sample of a dog barking in the background and it hijacks my monkey brain so fast. You cannot help but feel and see a dog when you hear it bark. And you can harness that to transport listeners to explicit places that the music does in a more ambiguous way.

It's also helpful to write from some of these sonic places. Like, we had the Zefrank sample which is from [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xJ9e32MNEOk). And I put that in there before we had vocals. I'm not even sure how much what Zefrank is saying and what Lindsey is saying tie together, but they both exist in the same melancholy, nostalgia, LCD fever dream and I know when we were writing, it was helpful to have that energy there as we were putting melodies down.

### Vocals

Lindsey's voice is so cool and we love layering it in fun and weird ways. You can get several textures out of one voice. Lindsey tracks different depending on where we want it to sit in the vocal stack. The "Very high harmony" track is her in a falsetto breathy take and then we pitched it up another octave, kind of hyperpop vibe. And out of context it sounds hilarious. But within context, it really adds something.

Here's basically what the processing looks like on vox.

<figure class="blog-media">
  <img src="img/mix-breakdown-ricky-ricardo/vox-processing.png" alt="Vocal processing chain">
</figure>

Pro-G for breath noise gate. Pro-DS for deesser. Autotune--well, you know, I will say that some people reading this might not realize how common Autotune is. For one, it's industry standard. But, for two, specifically this Antares Autotune plugin colors the voice in a really pleasing way that we've been conditioned to enjoy from pop music. I love the way it affects a voice. It's honestly less about purifying the note for me and more about chasing that color that I can't seem to get without it. Then UAD Neve1073 preamp, which I'm doing some EQing with. We tracked vox on a WA-14 and I'm really not used to this mic, so I was leaning on the Neve to get it sounding good to my ears.

Spiff helps with some breath and mouth noises. And then, the Howard Benson vox. Man, I was so skeptical of this plugin. It was suggested to my band High Press by Tim Mctague from Underoath and my initial thoughts were 1. Yeah, maybe it works for Underoath. But, really what more does Underoath need than extreme compression and saturation? 2. An all in one plugin feels like all in one shampoo, conditioner, body wash. Why would I use one plugin for compression, width, saturation, eq, reverb, delay when I could use the best of each of those plugins? And yet...it just fucking sounds good. It sounds good for Julep and High Press and Underoath. And it sounds good fast. Like, it just immediately gets the vocals close to what I'm chasing and when you're writing and arranging, sometimes that speed and ease is all you need.

I think an important part of this is the knowledge that the performance and arrangement is so much more important than any plugin. And so far I've only mixed Lindsey and Vagner/RJ from High Press with this and they are all incredible vocalists. So, maybe I'm just experiencing a frictionless mixing environment where this plugin can shine.
