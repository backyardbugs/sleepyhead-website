Let's look at some websites.

I wanted my own website after finding sights like:

- https://kottke.org
- https://www.robinsloan.com/lab/
- [For The Love of God, Make Your Own Website](https://aftermath.site/website-musk-twitter-facebook-internet/)

I remember a lot of shitty websites with singular purposes in the early 2000s. The internet felt more fun back then. Back when there was a website that existed just to show a spinning set of testicles and a counter every time they finished their rotation. Or the website purple.com which was just a purple page.

If you go to neocities home page you can explore a bunch of sites that remind me of these times:

- https://neocities.org/browse
- https://ita.toys/
- https://ribo.zone

There's just something really right about finding this person's zine about holding bugs: https://ribo.zone/bugs/holding

or this little poem about a king snail named Snilliam: https://sowe.li/snoems/

or (from the same site) this person's list of plants they wrote in 2023 https://sowe.li/lists/of_plants/

There's something delightful about those things that exist for that person and nothing more. There's no monetization happening. No commodification. it's just a person playing and being themselves and leaving mess.

So, that's one thing I want. For my site to be a space where I can play and be myself and leave a mess.

I also like seeing people work with the door open. Such as:

- https://jamesclear.com/annual-review
- https://makoism.com
- https://www.willwight.com/a-blog-of-dubious-intent

It's a little different, of course. But the bones of Kottke are there. Dark mode by default. A minimal, old internet feel. Unobtrusive. Maybe even a little boring. Clean.

I like the design. I think it feels good to visit my website. It makes me feel productive to look at it. Which, if you can't tell, is a feeling I am strongly desiring to have.

Yet, there are aspects of the design I want to change. My initial thought was that I wanted to make the site more interactive. So, I began with this drawing which I was thinking would be the home page:

<figure class="blog-media">
  <img src="img/website-design-audit/interactive-home-sketch.jpg" alt="Hand-drawn interactive homepage sketch">
</figure>

Each asset would have some kind of animated loop to it. The cig would smoke, the me in the hyperbolic recovery chamber thing would float a little, maybe the lamp would flicker. And you could click on each asset to be taken to a new page.

There's two problems with this design. 1. It'd look bad on mobile, where most people view the site. 2. It'd be a lot of work and I think it would compromise the feel of the site if I didn't implement this hand drawn feel throughout the whole thing. You'd click on it and then it'd take you to a much cleaner more sterile feel, which just didn't make sense to me.

So, I was having a hard time finishing it because I was worried about those two things. Then I saw this website: https://worm-blossom.org

And I thought, aha! Now, here is a site that feels warm and cozy and is still readable (though, less than I'd like to be honest). But it feels more organic. Less techy.

They're achieving this through hand drawn elements. Warm minimal pallet. Light mode. And no matter where you scroll on the page, there will be some of that hand drawn stuff there. Like if your website is a room, you want to dress the room up in some plants. Give it some life. They've also got a midi piano music player thing that is a little too clunky for my taste, but does support the aesthetic. And I also like this weekly format thing they've got going on. Though, my website is a bit more project oriented, so I don't want to integrate that.

So, I think my next step will be to try and draw some assets for my site and give it some of this life that worm blossom has. And then maybe after that I'll feel more excited about the interactive home page.

Anyways, I've written too much on this as is. I'm going to start drawing those assets! Thanks for reading. Ta ta.
