> "The hard truth of the situation is that the American oligarchy has become interested in cultural power."

<!--more-->

I feel like the American oligarchy is sort of hitting a ceiling of power. Poor chuds. They've claimed everything. And what can they claim now? Before Citizens United, there was a limit to the political power that economic power afforded you. And now, like a dog who caught the mailman, they've got both the economic and political power. And they used that political power to feed their economic power. And yippee! Now we have the world's first trillionaire!

But the dog must be fed. And next on the menu for the oligarchy, appears to be cultural power. I think arguably this is harder to achieve than economic or political power. Is it even possible to be powerful and cool? As an artist rises in power, there appears to be a diminishing return on their coolness level. I think about brands on X, the everything app, taking on a human/funny/satirical tone and how profoundly uncool it is. Brands are hungry for cultural power too.

I do wonder how absurd and strange culture is going to get as a response to the American oligarchy occupying the "cool" spaces. In some ways, I wonder how much of the "anti-woke" movement is the right's own response to the inauthenticity of brands and political entities masquerading as humans. In their own fucked up way, the right is trying to fight against the dominant culture. Unfortunately, they are cornballs, and their idea of subversive subculture is not far off from a 2010 Modern Warfare 2 lobby.

I don't think that the American Oligarchy will be able to claim cultural power, because I think the masses are craving a culture that is devoid of power. But I do think they are trying. And I think there will be a shift towards the strange as a result. When a brand co-opts something for their aesthetic, that shit becomes corny immediately. So that which is most culturally valuable, is that which a brand can't co-opt. This is one of the reasons I have a website and why I chose neocities as the place to host my website. Go to the neocities homepage and you will see a bunch of weird shit made by people. Humans being humans. Humans being cringe. You know what you won't see here? A fucking burger king website.

I have a lot more thoughts on this and specifically this idea of taste being a core skill as AI approaches making human work obsolete. Per the video, Percia Verlin quotes Kyle Chayka, who herself is quoting Voltaire, "In order to have taste, it is not enough to see and to know what is beautiful in a given work. One must feel beauty and be moved by it. Then Kyle says, No large language model has yet been programmed to feel anything, and no number of branded baseball caps is going to change that."

anyways, watch the video. It's great.
